barca-possession · how a frame gets its team today

Possession state machine

The bench showed that MatchLens gives no team on 18–20% of the frames where the ball is visible and play is live. The scorer then copies the previous team into those frames, which carries the old possession past a turnover. This page draws the exact logic that produces that, as it is in the code, before we research fixes.

Layer 0 · applies to every frame first

The gate before possession

Every frame passes two checks before any possession logic runs. Either one stops the possession tracker for that frame.

Turning off the "ball unseen 0.5 s means dead" rule (loop iteration 1) only fixed the first check. The second check is inside the possession tracker itself (possession.py:354): every frame without a ball position resets the possessor to nobody. After a 20-second gap the tracker starts again from zero and needs a new proven gain before it names any team.

Layer 1 · MatchLens (footballtactics/backend/events/possession.py)

MatchLens possession tracker

A port of Vidal-Codina et al. (2022) §2.3. It works per player. Each frame is first classified, then the state moves. The frame's label is the team of current_possessor_id; when that is empty the frame has no answer.

"Contact proven" (_sequence_indicates_contact)

  • The ball's direction at the start of P's control run and at its latest frame differ: dot product of the two unit vectors below 0.85 (about 32°), or
  • at some frame in the run, incoming and outgoing speed differ by more than 1.5 m/s.
  • Both read a Savitzky-Golay smoothed ball track (window 7, order 2). If the ball looks airborne, the bar is raised (0.75 and 2.25 m/s).
  • A duel winner skips this test: winning a duel counts as a proven gain.
Why frames lose their team

Five ways a live frame ends up with no team

#PathWhen it happens in footballEffect after the scorer's fill
1Candidate P, contact not yet provenA receiver traps a slow pass cleanly: little change of direction or speed, so the test fails for several frames, sometimes the whole control run.Old team carried forward. On an interception that is the wrong team until the gain is proven.
2Held by P, then Q controlsEvery pass or tackle. P's loss is emitted at once; Q starts as a candidate.Same as 1: the frames between Q's first touch and Q's proven gain carry P's team.
3Pending loss, 3 s with no controlA long ball or a loose ball nobody reaches quickly.Idle until someone's gain is proven.
4Duel without the previous holderA 50-50 between two players who did not have the ball.No team until the duel ends with a winner.
5PAUSE on any frame without a ball positionEvery ball gap, which in our match is 41% of graded frames, mostly in blocks of 5–30 s.The holder is forgotten; after the gap the next gain must be proven from scratch.

Measured on the external bench (visible-ball live frames, Metrica G1/G2): MatchLens named the right team on 68–72%, gave no team on 18–20%, called the ball dead on 2–8%, and named the wrong team on only 6%. The problem is mostly silence at the wrong moment, not wrong answers.

Bench numbers: scripts/diag_visible_team.py, loop iteration 2 (.planning/LOOP.md).

Layer 1, current replacement · src/possess/team_control.py

Our team-level rule (loop iteration 2)

What the pipeline uses now instead of the per-player tracker. It works per team, keeps the current team through anything ambiguous, and never resets on a missing ball. Tuned on external data: zone 1.0 m, 5 frames.

stateDiagram-v2
  direction LR
  [*] --> Unknown
  Unknown: No team yet (start of match)
  HoldT: Team T has it
  Challenge: Team U challenging · n of 5
  Dead: Dead ball

  Unknown --> Challenge: a team alone in its 1.0 m zone
  HoldT --> HoldT: T in zone, duel, nobody in zone, or ball hidden
  HoldT --> Challenge: U alone in its zone (n = 1)
  Challenge --> Challenge: U alone in zone again (n + 1), or ambiguous frame (n kept)
  Challenge --> HoldT: T alone in zone (challenge dropped)
  Challenge --> HoldU: n reaches 5
  HoldU: Team U has it
  HoldT --> Dead: dead-ball detector
  Challenge --> Dead: dead-ball detector (challenge dropped)
  Dead --> HoldT: play resumes (team kept from before)

What it fixed

  • No empty labels during live play once a team has been named, so no late carry-over from silence.
  • Ball gaps keep the team (possession survives about 75% of gaps).
  • Tune spellF1 0.326 → 0.356 (iteration 2), 0.375 with the new dead-ball detector (iteration 3).

What it still gets wrong

  • The 5 control frames are counted, not required in a row: frames where nobody is in a zone do not reset the count. The code comment says "consecutive"; the code does not do that.
  • It still decides on distance alone. There is no touch evidence, so a defender standing next to a passing lane can win the ball on paper.
  • A turnover hidden inside a ball gap is only seen when the ball comes back, and is dated then.
  • On IDSSE it switches too rarely: 286 possessions predicted against 511.
  • After a stoppage the team before the stoppage is kept, though the restart may belong to the other team.

Half of all true possessions last under 4 seconds; touch detection is what finally catches them. Next: clean the ball track (tracking jumps fire false touches) and turnovers while the ball is hidden. Earlier: tune 0.3586 → 0.4932, held-out 0.3556 → 0.4789 with team shape. Team shape is the biggest single gain and it held up on the held-out matches (+0.116), under 10 cm position noise, and with 7–12 players per team. Tune numbers for the learned models are leave-one-match-out. Every number was reproduced by an independent Fable review. The public 5-minute dip (0.444 → 0.433) is the same 19 possessions against 17, within one-possession noise.

Next

Questions the research should answer

  1. How should a gain be confirmed without waiting: touch evidence, retention time, passes completed by the new team, or a mix?
  2. How should the tracker carry its belief through a ball gap instead of resetting, and how should it date a turnover found after the gap?
  3. Should the decision be made frame by frame at all, or as a whole-match segmentation (HMM or HSMM) where these rules become evidence?
  4. Who restarts after a stoppage: can the restart team be read from positions or from Smart Tagging set pieces?
  5. What do published systems (Vidal-Codina, Link and Hoernig's ball possession, SkillCorner, Metrica's own corrected tracking) do for each of these?
