## 1. Approach in one paragraph
We label every frame from the tactical tracking, plus two uses of Smart Tagging: the "Kick Off (Start)" tags, to find each half's kick-off, and the goal kick, corner and throw-in clips, only as a gate for finding the physical restart (ADR-029). Per frame we compute ID-free features (distance from the ball to the nearest home and away player, team centroid speeds, anonymous player positions), detect stoppages, and then decode each half **as a whole** with a 4-state Viterbi model (Home, Away, Dead-after-Home, Dead-after-Away) instead of deciding frame by frame. Evidence enters the decoder as additive scores: how much nearer one team is to the ball, a frame rule with retention, ball touches near one team, the ball's direction of flight (the team attacking in the direction the ball travels), a small learned model of which team holds the visible ball, team-shape models that say which team has the ball (applied on every frame, most useful while the ball is hidden), and stoppage evidence. Switching team, entering and leaving a stoppage all carry costs; leaving a stoppage toward the other team is cheaper, because the other team restarts after 78–90% of stoppages on external truth. After decoding, two controlled-possession passes give back a team spell that interrupts the other team's live spell when it lasts under 0.6 s (ADR-030), or under 2 s without one clean touch of its own (the organisers' "a touch without control is not a change"); then a stoppage's start is moved to the ball's line crossing, and a ball seen beyond a line for 2 s or more is dead (Law 9). Three dead-ball steps cover a ball that is visible but dead: a small learned model of "the visible ball is dead" adds to the stoppage evidence (ADR-028), a ball at rest for 3 s or more with no player within 1 m is dead (ADR-028), and inside a tagged goal kick, corner or throw-in the frames from our last dead evidence to the physical restart are dead (ADR-029; adopted as a recorded exception to our gate, §10). All parameters and models were chosen on other matches; nothing was fitted on the provided match.

## 2. Why this design (what the data told us)
| Finding | Evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The ball is missing in long blocks | 99.9% of missing ball frames are in gaps of 5 s or more | Interpolating the ball cannot help (10 fill methods scored, none beat the unfilled ball; 5 more only tested for position error); possession must be decided without the ball |
| A missing ball is not a dead ball | external truth: possession usually survives a gap; MatchLens's "ball unseen 0.5 s → dead" over-split possessions | removed that rule |
| Who has the ball matters more than when play stops | oracle ablations on external matches | team evidence first, stoppages second |
| Half of all possessions last under 4 s on external truth | 588 of 1,198 | touch detection for deflections, tackles, clearances |
| The external truth counts deflections as possessions; the organisers do not | CHALLENGE.md "Change of possession"; ADR-022 | controlled-possession post-pass (`ctl_s`: 1.0 s in ADR-022, 0.6 s since ADR-030) and a second, relabelled external truth (§6) |
| The other team restarts after most stoppages | 82% / 78% / 90% on three external matches | restart prior in the decoder |
| The provided ball track is low-passed (acceleration p99 30 and 47 m/s² per half, against 204–619 on raw external tracks) | input statistics only | touch detection adapted to smoothed tracks (§3, §8 compliance notes) |

## 3. Pipeline (`src/possess/`)
1. **Read** the EPTS tracking (kloppy line reader, streamed): team slots, goalkeeper guess, attacking direction per half by vote. (`epts.py`, `features.py`, `shape.py`)
2. **Kick-offs** from Smart Tagging: the "Kick Off (Start)" tag window of each half; the kick-off is the frame in that window where the ball is nearest the centre spot. Graded frames before it are D and the decoder starts the half there. The only other tags read are the goal kick, corner and throw-in clips, used in step 8. (`kickoff.py`, `pipeline.run_challenge`)
3. **Stoppages**: MatchLens dead-ball detector ported (out of bounds, stationary or placed ball, hysteresis; missing-ball rule removed) combined with ball-far-from-everyone AND (near-a-line OR collective slowdown), and a team-shape stoppage model that also reads the speed of the fast players. On hidden-ball frames its log-odds are shifted by a per-match prior estimated without labels (§8). On visible frames a learned "the visible ball is dead" logistic model (21 team-blind input features) is added to the stoppage log-odds (`w_bd = 1.0`, ADR-028); its constants are stored in the repository (`balldead.MODEL`) and were fitted only on the external tuning matches. (`stoppage_ml.py`, `deadball2.py`, `shape.py`, `balldead.py`)
4. **Ball cleaning** for touch detection: drop teleports and short frozen runs (the off-pitch rule exists but is off: `off_m=None`). (`ballclean.py`)
5. **Team evidence**: soft distance score capped at the 1 m control zone; frame rule with 5-frame retention and back-dated switches; team-level touch rule ported from MatchLens's impulse criterion (radius in §6); a touch hold when the ball reappears at one team's feet after a hidden block; team-shape model on every frame (`shape_vis = 1.0`); **ball flight direction** on visible frames: the team attacking in the ball's direction of travel gets evidence `w_fly = 0.7`, ramped in between 4 and 8 m/s of ball speed (`decode.flight_dir`, ADR-024); **learned ball-team evidence** on visible frames: a logistic model of which team holds the visible ball from 21 input-only features (proximity now and over 0.5–4 s, team at the last impulse, flight, zone, shape), weight `w_bt = 0.5`, neutral toward D. Its constants are stored in the repository (`ballteam.MODEL`, reproducible with `scripts/a8b_ml.py`); it was fitted only on the external tuning matches, scored leave-one-match-out, and never saw the provided match (ADR-026). (`decode.py`, `team_control.py`, `shape.py`, `ballteam.py`)
6. **Decode** each half with Viterbi. A switch inside a hidden-ball block is **not** moved to a fixed point: the default sets `gap_pos = -1.0` (`shape.DEC_SHAPE`), which overrides `DEC_BASE`'s 0.5, so `decode.place_gap_switches` is not called and the switch stays where the Viterbi path puts it, i.e. where the team-shape scores change (`w_shape = 0.05`, applied on all frames), or at the Viterbi tie-break when they are flat. (`gap_pos = 0.5`, mid-gap, applies only without player positions, which never happens on the provided match.) (`decode.py`)
7. **Post-passes**, in this order: (a) the end of every hidden stoppage of 3 s or more is moved to the largest step-up of the fast-player speed within ±2 s (`edges.py`, `ed_exit = 1`); (b) the controlled-possession pass `ctl_s = 0.6`: a team spell under 0.6 s that follows the other team's live spell goes back (`ctl_truth.relabel`, ADR-022; 1.0 s until ADR-030); (c) the no-clean-touch pass `ctl_hi = 2.0, ctl_touch = 1`: a team spell under 2 s that follows the other team's live spell goes back unless it holds at least one uncontested touch of its own team (ADR-023); (d) line-crossing stoppage start `ed_cross = 5.0, ed_cross_strict = 1`: a predicted stoppage's entry moves to the nearest ball line crossing within 5 s (ADR-025); (e) visible dead ball `ed_xdead = 1, ed_xdead_min = 2.0`: a visible ball more than 0.5 m beyond a line, with no stoppage nearby, for 2 s or more is D until it is seen inside the pitch again (ADR-025); (f) still ball `ed_still = 1.0`: a ball at rest for 3 s or more with no player within 1 m is D (`edges.still_dead`, ADR-028). (d), (e) and (f) learn nothing. (`decode.decode_halves`, `edges.py`)
8. **Set-piece restarts** (`sp = 1, sp_hid = 1, sp_take_s = 0` in `pipeline.TAGS`, ADR-029): for each Smart Tagging goal kick, corner or throw-in clip, look from 3 s before to 15 s after the clip start for the physical restart: the ball at rest on the spot the Laws require and then kicked, or reappearing there and moving. Frames from our last dead evidence (within 15 s) to that restart become D. No restart found: nothing changes. Free kicks are excluded (no fixed spot). The tag is a gate, not a clock; its time and Team field are not used as labels. (`setpiece.py`, `pipeline.setpiece_rows`)
9. **Write** 199,319 rows, `X` outside the graded halves. (`pipeline.py`)

## 7. Possession rules implemented (summary)
- A frame is D when the stoppage evidence says so; a missing ball alone never makes a frame dead. Graded frames before a half's kick-off are D. A visible ball beyond a line for 2 s or more is D, and a stoppage starts at the ball's line crossing when one is seen within 5 s. A visible ball that a learned model calls dead, or that rests for 3 s or more with no player within 1 m, is D. Inside a tagged goal kick, corner or throw-in, the frames up to the physical restart are D.
- A team gains the ball by being alone near it for several frames (1 m control zone), or by a touch (radius as in §6); a touch keeps the team in possession until the next touch, at most 4 s. A ball reappearing at one team's feet after a hidden block is that team's touch.
- Duels, loose balls and hidden balls keep the current team unless team-shape, flight-direction or ball-team evidence says otherwise.
- After a stoppage the decoder prefers the other team, but player and ball evidence decide.
- A team spell under 0.6 s that interrupts the other team's live spell is not a change of possession; nor is one under 2 s without a clean touch of its own.

The stoppage model also reads the speed of the fast players (90th percentile of all tracked players' speeds, 1 s and 5 s centred means): a dead ball shows up as the whole pitch slowing down, which sharpens where hidden stoppages start and end. **Second realistic bench (`real2`).** The provided input has a smoothed ball track, a ball that disappears during stoppages and play restarts, and players that drop out of the tracking while the ball is hidden. `real2` adds these to `real1`: Gaussian-smoothed ball (sigma 7 frames), hiding blocks that start on true stoppages (from a slow ball) and end at the restart kick with lengths drawn from the provided gap list, and player loss inside hidden blocks. Its constants come from aggregates of the provided match's input only, never its labels. On `real2` the default scored 0.430 before the next change; several earlier gains shrink (the touch detector adds +0.072 on `real1` but −0.024 on `real2`). From here on a change is adopted only if `real1` and `real2` do not drop by more than 0.003 and at least one gains.

**Touch detection on low-passed ball tracks.** A smoothed ball blurs the sharp velocity change the touch detector looks for. When a half's ball track is low-passed (acceleration 99th percentile below 150 m/s²), touch detection uses 8-frame velocities, one touch per velocity-change peak and a 2 m radius. `real2` rises from 0.430 to 0.483 (second seed 0.433 to 0.482; paired bootstrap +0.053 and +0.049, 90% intervals above zero, all six match-seed pairs up); `real1` and the original bench do not change because the mode is off there. On the provided match the share of hold time that comes from touches rises from 0.27 to 0.67 and possessions per half hour from 112.5 to 119.0. At that step the public window moved 0.529 to 0.506 (17 vs 17 possessions); it is reported only and was not used to choose.

*Compliance note.* This branch exists because of the provided input and fires only on it, so we disclose it. The mode switch is a threshold on the ball's acceleration: unsmoothed external tracks have a 99th percentile of 204–619 per half, `real2` 36–41, the provided match 30 and 47. Any threshold between 48 and 204 gives identical output on every dataset, so the value inside that gap is not a fitted parameter. The window length and radius were chosen on external `real2` truth; lag and sigma scans all gained. No label of the provided match was used.

**Per-match dead-ball prior on hidden frames.** The stoppage model carries the dead-ball share of the training matches as its prior, which is too low for a match where the ball is hidden for a large share of the time. For each match we estimate the dead-ball share on hidden-ball frames without labels, by expectation-maximisation on the stoppage model's own posteriors (Saerens et al. 2002, prior-shift adaptation). The hidden-frame log-odds are shifted by logit(estimate) minus logit(training prior), and hidden frames with no shape evidence get the estimated prior. The shift is applied only if it is at least 0.6 in size and the match has at least 40,000 hidden frames with evidence (it was validated on full matches only; per half, random hiding gives shifts up to 1.4). Any threshold between 0.49 and 0.97 gives identical output on every dataset. On `real2` the score rises from 0.483 to 0.517 (second seed 0.482 to 0.515; paired bootstrap +0.034, 90% interval +0.024 to +0.045); `real1` and the original bench do not change because the shift gate stays shut there. Predicted against true hidden dead-ball share on `real2` is 0.727/0.714, 0.819/0.826 and 0.838/0.800. On the provided match the estimate is 0.869 (shift +2.27), the hidden dead-ball share goes from 0.724 to about 0.897 and dead-ball runs per half hour from 24.4 to 30.0. The provided match lies slightly beyond `real2` on every input indicator we checked, in the same direction (hidden share 0.41, median hidden block 10.7 s, fast-player speed 1.61 m/s), so this is a mild extrapolation; a +1.0 log-odds overestimate on `real2` still scores 0.515.

*Compliance note.* This is inference, not fitting: no label, constant or threshold comes from the provided match, and the estimate is recomputed per match in the same way for every match, like our attacking-direction vote. The method is however literally a prior recalibration on the match being scored, so we disclose it here. The two input-adaptive settings: `sd_prior` shifts the stoppage model's hidden-frame log-odds to the dead-ball share estimated on the match's own hidden frames (this paragraph); `smooth_touch` switches touch detection to the low-passed mode when the ball track's acceleration p99 is below 150 m/s² (above). Both use only input statistics of the match, never its labels. We asked the organisers about both; no answer was received before submission, so both stay on as described.

**Reappearing ball (L2-22, ADR-019).** On `real2` the ball often vanishes near a player and reappears on a kick, where the impulse test is blind (it needs visible frames on both sides). The first visible frame after a hidden block of at least 1 s starts a touch hold for the only team within 1 m (MatchLens control zone). One configuration, no grid. `real2` 0.5173 → 0.5326 (5 seeds pooled +0.0090, CI90 [+0.0055, +0.0126]; 15/15 seed × match cells ≥ 0). Cost: `real1` 0.6081 → 0.6068, `orig` 0.6305 → 0.6283 (orig loss CI90 [−0.0048, −0.0004], inside the 0.005 tolerance). It fires on 77 of 131 hidden blocks of the provided match.

**Touch at the contact point (L3-A4, ADR-020).** On a heavily low-passed track the detected touch point sits about 2 m from the player's feet, so a touch is credited to nobody or contested. `decode.touch_vertex` rebuilds the contact point as the meeting point of the straight incoming and outgoing ball flight (Vidal-Codina §2.3.3) and credits a team only when it is the only team within 2 m (both within = contested). It runs only when the acceleration p99 is below 70 m/s² (external sigma 5–7 tracks ≤ 57, sigma 3 ≥ 89; chosen on external data). `real2` 0.5326 → 0.5618 (6 seeds pooled +0.029, CI90 [+0.017, +0.042]); `real1` and `orig` byte-identical. Same compliance note as the low-passed touch mode.

**Graded frames before the kick-off (ADR-021).** See §6 "Halves". The constant 113,742 is not used; the kick-off comes from the tag window and the centre spot. External benches are unaffected.

**Controlled possession (ADR-022).** See §6 and the table above. The value 1.0 s was the user's choice from external numbers only (2.0 s loses up to −0.030 on the T = 1 truth and −0.065 on the old truth); ADR-030 later lowered it to 0.6 s (above).

## 9. Limitations
- **Definitions still approximated** (§6): D from proxies rather than the whistle (line crossings only where the ball is seen); control = 0.6 s, or 2 s without a clean touch, rather than "a few seconds", and no pass counting; one clean but uncontrolled touch can still yield an opponent spell over 0.6 s; restart team inferred, not read from set-piece tags; no rule for goal → kick-off or for free kicks; no ball height for aerial duels.
- **Set-piece rule (ADR-029) fails our strict gate** and is in the default only as a recorded exception (§10). Known open defects, not fixed before submission: the goal-area half-width in `setpiece.py` is 14.66 m where it should be 9.16 m (the goal-kick spot test is too wide); the throw-in spot has no outer bound; a later "ball at rest" restart wins over an earlier "ball reappears" one; one 2-frame home spell is left inside a stoppage on the provided match. Cold QA checked the largest change (12.6 s) and found a ball at rest on the goal-area line, not live play.
- **Visible dead ball (ADR-028), cold-QA caveats:** the still-ball rule gains only on `orig` and adds 166–291 wrong D frames on the other benches; the learned model loses on one match (IDSSE 1, −0.0042 on `real2` seed 0); on `real2` seed 0 the interval includes zero.
- **Date of a change.** We date a turnover at its first touch, like the external truth; if the organisers date it at control, our switches after duels sit early, and no external data can check this (L11-B). Tested on the final day (L26): the dating convention (first touch vs control) changes our external score by up to 0.09, while no variant of ours is better under every convention (stake between variants ≤ 0.004). Several teams' public write-ups read the public clip as dating at the first touch, which is what we do.
- **Generality (`docs/generality-code-audit.md`).** (1) The entry point runs this one match: frame count and graded halves are constants from the organisers' data description, and frame rate and pitch size are constants too. (2) Built for a tactical feed with nearly all players tracked and a calibrated pitch (line rules at 0.5 m); not tested on broadcast tracking. (3) Two regime switches read the ball track's smoothing (acceleration p99 against 150 and 70 m/s²); the second separates a gain from a loss with one calibration point on each side, and this match clears it narrowly. (4) The hidden-frame prior switches on only above 40,000 hidden frames with shape evidence: a hard step. (5) The kick-off and set-piece rules depend on this Smart Tagging product (its window and fill come from this file's input inventory); without a tag file both rules are lost. (6) After decoding, the line, still-ball and set-piece post-passes can only add D; the control pass is not run again. (7) The benches that gated every step since L2-20 imitate this match's input statistics (41% hidden, gap histogram, smoothing); no labels were used, but the selection environment is this match's.
- **The 2 s control rule is judged on a truth built with T = 2 s**, so part of its gain is by construction; it is about zero if the organisers' threshold is under 2 s (ADR-023).
- **Learned ball-team model (ADR-026), cold-QA caveats:** some features look 0.2–0.6 s ahead (centred velocity); the training matches' shape feature comes from a shape model that saw the test match (second order); the provided match is more one-sided (59.6% home) than any tuning match. Expected real gain about +0.009.
- **Error types in the public clip** (draft 17d, 994 of 7,500 frames differ; ADR-027, diagnosis only): a visible dead ball labelled as a team for 18.7 s (one 10.5 s visible stoppage); an opponent spell that starts early or lasts long (6.4 s) plus an extra 3.4 s spell; one 6.6 s hidden block missed; short spells under 1.5 s missed or extra. Not acted on unless a fix first shows the same error class on external data; the visible dead-ball class led to ADR-028 and ADR-029, the others are still open.
- **Hidden turnovers are placed by the shape scores**, not snapped to a feature. Three attempts to time them better on external data (flow change point, directional discount, earlier shift; T88–T90) were rejected or not merged.
- **Hidden D share rose on the provided match.** The fast-player speed feature raises the share of hidden-ball frames called dead from 0.673 to 0.711 (0.724 after the restart snap), and the per-match prior to about 0.897, while hidden-ball frames there are genuinely slow (median fast-player speed 1.62 m/s against 3.38 m/s visible); over-calling dead ball is a residual risk.
- **Held-out set looked at 7 times** (all before submission), so the held-out numbers are slightly optimistic; the steps from ADR-017 on were not measured on it.
- **Duel rule is near-inert on the provided match.** Contested touches are rare there (0.16 per minute against 1.6–3.0 on the benches).
- **Bench realism.** The honest expectation for the provided match is `real2`-like. Stoppage detection on a moving dead ball is the weakest part.
- **Rejected idea, kept for the record:** a still-ball stoppage rule added +0.028 on the bench but would not transfer (a still ball is 4.5% of visible frames on the real match vs 21–42% on the bench).
- Tuning data is clean 11 v 11 tracking; robustness to 7–12 noisy players was simulated, not measured on real automatic tracking.
- Metrica event-derived truth differs from corrected-tracking truth; absolute scores will not transfer exactly.

## 10. Use of AI tools and compliance
**Public clip used for diagnosis (ADR-027, disclosed here).** On 17 Nov, after ADR-026 (draft 17d), we listed the *types* of error our draft makes on the public 5-minute clip (for example "a visible dead ball labelled as a team"), not the frames' answers. Agents could read that list and look at the tracking input at those moments to form ideas. They never opened the truth file. Any idea had first to show the same error type on the external matches, and every mechanism and constant was chosen and gated on external data only; the public score is reported after a decision, never used to pick between variants. Two changes came from this diagnosis: the visible dead ball (ADR-028), which passed our gate on external data, and the set-piece rule (ADR-029), requested by the user after seeing a goal-kick error (truth dead for about 10 s, we labelled Barcelona) in the viewer on the public clip. Both were built, validated and gated on external matches (ADR-029 with simulated tags), and the public score was computed only after the decision.

**Recorded exception to our adoption gate (ADR-029).** The set-piece rule fails our strict gate: on `real2`, our main external bench, the median gain is about +0.002 against the +0.005 we required, and two of four seeds have intervals that include zero; `real1` and `orig` pass and no bench drops. The user decided to adopt it anyway, and we record the reason: `real2` hides the ball during every stoppage, so a rule that needs the ball visible at a restart can barely act there, while on the provided input the ball is visible in the first 3 s of 80% of goal-kick clips (measured on the input only, not on labels). Cold QA reproduced the numbers bit-identically and accepted it as a recorded exception.

Code was written with Claude Code agents. Agents read the provided raw data only after the organisers' written clarification ("Yes you can use agents to read raw data", 11 Nov 2026), which states that its rules take precedence over earlier information; the organisers' earlier FAQ text forbids uploading competition material to hosted LLMs, and we relied on the later email. No labels were created by hand; nothing was fitted on the provided game.
